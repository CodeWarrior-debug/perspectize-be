## Design Context

### Product

**Perspectize** — a platform for storing, refining, and sharing perspectives on content (YouTube videos first). A user pastes a video, writes what they think of it (a quick agree/disagree, or a full 0–1000 quality rating with a long-form review), and later browses their own and others' perspectives. The product's single UX mantra is:

> **Effortless to input. Calm to browse.**

It is designed as the deliberate opposite of social media. Users should never feel overwhelmed, lost, interrupted, or out of control. There are no streaks, no notification badges, no trending, no autoplay, no infinite scroll.

### Users

Two audiences, one interface:

- **Heavy users — niche community of thoughtful curators.** People who care about quality opinions and carefully refined perspectives. They return to the app to write long reviews, compare their past takes, and build a personal canon. The interface should feel earned and rewarding the longer they use it.
- **General users — curious newcomers.** People who just watched a video and want to jot down what they thought. They should find the app warm, curious, and conversational — never institutional or intimidating. First-time friction must be near-zero.

**Context of use:** mostly unhurried. Weeknight wind-down, weekend-morning coffee, post-viewing reflection. Rarely during frantic multitasking. Design for the reader who has already decided to pay attention.

### Brand Personality

**Calm and precise, beautiful, with touches of cleverness and humor.**

Decoded:

- **Calm** — whitespace is a primary design element. Nothing hurries the user. Interactions resolve smoothly, never theatrically.
- **Precise** — every number aligned, every label considered, every margin intentional. Precision is how the product earns trust from the curator audience.
- **Beautiful** — this is a product people should *want* to look at. Typography carries weight. Small details are lavished on. It should feel closer to a well-printed magazine than to a SaaS dashboard.
- **Clever / humorous** — wit lives in the copy and in unexpected small moments, not in splashy motion or emoji. Empty states, microcopy, page titles, and error messages all get a dry, warm voice. The wireframe glasses logo should feel like it's raising an eyebrow.

**Voice reference:** *The New Yorker* caption writer, or the placard next to an exhibit at a small natural history museum — confident, a little literary, never pompous, occasionally funny when you're not expecting it.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Editorial reference-work.** Think: a reading-room catalogue, a printed index at the back of a book, an archivist's software. The interface is a container for thought, not a performer competing for attention.

**Core aesthetic moves:**

- **Dual-font system stays** — Geist for UI chrome (precise, quiet sans), Charter for all user-written content and long reading passages (serif, literary). The sans/serif contrast is the product's typographic signature and must never be flattened into a single family.
- **Content is sovereign** — chrome recedes; user-written perspectives are set in generous Charter with real measure (~65–75ch), real leading, and room to breathe. The perspective is the hero object, the table is just a way to find it.
- **Asymmetry over card-grids** — resist the reflex to wrap everything in a rounded rectangle. Layouts should use whitespace, alignment, and typography to create hierarchy rather than outlining every block.
- **Numerals are jewelry** — ratings, scores, dates, counts are the recurring small elements of the interface. Treat them like type specimens: tabular figures, thoughtful alignment, restrained color. When a 0–1000 score appears, it should feel satisfying to look at.
- **Color is a signature, not a shout** — the default palette is the navy + neutral paper established in the design spec. Rating colors (green/amber/red/slate) appear only on the specific elements they describe; they never bleed into chrome.
- **Motion is subtle, decelerating** — 150–200ms, exponential ease-out. Motion confirms causality, it does not perform.
- **Cleverness lives in copy and micro-details** — empty states, 404s, loading states, and system messages are the places to be playful. Not layouts, not animations.

### Theme System (First-Class Feature)

Perspectize ships a **theme picker with several curated themes**. Theme is not a boring light/dark toggle — it's part of the product's personality and part of what users can customize about their reading environment. Each theme is a considered artifact, not an auto-generated dark variant.

**Constraints on any theme:**

- Must keep the dual-font Geist / Charter system intact.
- Must maintain WCAG AA contrast for body text.
- Must tint neutrals toward the theme's primary hue (use OKLCH; reduce chroma near lightness extremes).
- Rating colors (positive / neutral / negative / undecided) must remain perceptually distinct in every theme but are free to be re-expressed in that theme's palette.
- Must feel like a coherent *place*, not a recolor. Each theme should have a name and a one-line description that evokes its mood.

**Direction for curating initial themes** (examples, not a locked list — pick intentionally when building the picker):

| Name (working)   | Mood                                          | Base                              |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **Reading Room** | Current default. Navy on warm paper-white.    | `#1a365d` navy + near-white paper |
| **Archive**      | Sepia, cotton rag, warm graphite text.        | Muted ochre + ink brown           |
| **Garden**       | Herbarium green, field-guide quiet.           | Deep moss + bone white            |
| **Midnight**     | Late-night long-form review writing.          | Deep navy/ink + warm off-white    |
| **Terminal**     | Power-user. Precise, dense, quietly nostalgic.| Near-black + phosphor tint        |

When implementing the picker: let users preview themes live, not via a modal. The picker itself is a moment to be beautiful — treat it as a small exhibit, not a settings row.

### Explicit Anti-References

**DO NOT look like:**

- A social media platform of any kind. No avatar feeds, no engagement metrics, no "Recently active," no algorithmic discovery chrome, no like counts as ambient decoration.
- A generic SaaS dashboard. No sidebar + metric-card hero, no purple-to-blue gradients, no Inter, no Lucide icons above every heading as visual filler.
- A YouTube clone. Thumbnails exist because the content is YouTube videos, but the chrome must never echo YouTube's own UI. No red accents, no subscribe-button shapes.
- A review site. No stars, no aggregate "Rotten Tomatoes"-style hero scores, no five-dots UI. The rating system is 0–1000 and agree/disagree, and it should look like nothing else.

### Design Principles

Five principles. Every design decision in this project must be defensible against these.

1. **Calm by construction.** Pause-aware pagination over infinite scroll. Stable, predictable layouts over surprising ones. Stillness over motion. If a pattern could plausibly appear in a social app, assume it's wrong and find another.

2. **Content is sovereign.** The user's perspective — their review, their claim, their rating — is the hero object. Chrome, containers, and navigation must recede. Never wrap a perspective in a decorated card that competes with the words inside it.

3. **Editorial typography carries the weight.** Geist and Charter, used with real hierarchy and real measure, do the work that cards and color would do in a lesser product. Before reaching for a border or a background tint, try size, weight, leading, and space.

4. **Cleverness lives in the small.** Humor and personality appear in copy, in microcopy, in empty states, in 404s, in the way a number is set, in the glasses-logo that seems to be looking at you. Never in theatrical animations, confetti, or decorative gradients.

5. **Theme as identity, not skin.** Every curated theme is a place with a mood. Themes are not reversible overlays on a base UI — they are intentional reinterpretations. When a user picks "Archive" they should feel they've walked into a different room, not toggled a dark-mode switch.

## Quick Reference for Future Design Work

When invoking an `impeccable` skill (`craft`, `polish`, `simplify`, `normalize`, `colorize`, etc.) on this project, it will read this file first. Always honor:

- **Fonts:** Geist (UI) + Charter (body/user content). Never flatten to one family. Never substitute Inter, Fraunces, or any font in the impeccable reflex-reject list.
- **Color:** OKLCH. Neutrals tinted toward the current theme's hue. Rating colors only on the rating elements themselves.
- **No forbidden patterns:** no `border-left` accent stripes, no gradient text, no glassmorphism, no infinite scroll, no card-in-card nesting.
- **Microcopy voice:** dry, warm, literary, occasionally funny. Never corporate, never exclamation marks, never "Oops!".
- **Theme-aware:** never hardcode a hex value that assumes the default theme. Everything routes through tokens.
